There was an old man
Whom everyone called the asphalt gardener
He grew neither roses nor tulips
He cultivated the cracks in the pavement
Every morning he went out with a small watering can
And watered the wild little weeds
The stubborn dandelions
The small nameless flowers growing between the cobblestones
People made fun of him
But he smiled
He said
Life is stronger than concrete
Beauty is more stubborn than ugliness
One day the town hall decided to repave the walk
They covered everything with new smooth asphalt
The old man was sad
But the following spring
A small yellow flower broke through the tar
Right in front of his door
And the old man began to water again.
