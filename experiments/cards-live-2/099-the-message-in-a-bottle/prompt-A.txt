Reproduce: the granular lithographic stipple texture covering every surface, the soft airbrushed color fields, the fine hatched line work, and the whimsical elongated caricature figures. Everything stays matte and printmade — visible lithographic grain in every color field, no smooth digital shading, no gloss, no soft light effects, no glowing outlines. NO FAKE AGING: no yellowed or browned paper, no stains, no foxing, no coffee marks, no dirt, no torn or deckled edges, no vignetting, no scratches, no creases, no simulated antique print. Palette of a 1920s lithographic poster: deep sea green dominating the scene, dark midnight blue as the second accent, and warm ochres — one low ochre sun on the water, a warm glint on wet sand — colors laid dark, saturated and velvety, never pale, never dirty.

Never generic cartoon heads.

Now the NEW subject, drawn in exactly that technique — one single emblematic motif, not a narrative scene: one heavy green glass bottle lying on its side on wet sand, monumental, filling most of the block, its cork gone and its mouth turned toward the viewer; inside it, clearly visible through the thick glass, a small curled scrap of paper bleached completely blank, not one mark left on it. Deep behind and far above, small and quiet along the top of the block: the flat dark line of an empty sea and one low ochre sun. Nothing else — generous calm dark space around the bottle, no footprints anywhere on the sand.

No text, no letters, no words anywhere — not even illegible squiggles. No signature, no edition number. No watermark, no sparkle or star symbol in any corner. Tender absurd humor, no kitsch. Portrait orientation 2:3.
