Study the attached reference lithographs by Adolf Born and imitate their TECHNIQUE precisely — not their subjects. Reproduce: the granular lithographic stipple texture, the soft airbrushed color fields, the fine hatched line work. Everything stays matte and printmade — visible lithographic grain, no smooth digital shading, no gloss, no soft light effects, no glowing outlines. Print it on clean, fresh, evenly toned pale paper. NO FAKE AGING: no yellowed or browned paper, no stains, no foxing, no coffee marks, no dirt, no torn or deckled edges, no vignetting, no scratches, no creases, no simulated antique print. Palette of a 1920s lithographic poster: deep burnt sienna rust-brown dominating the scene, bottle green as the second accent, and warm golden ochres — ochre lamplight along the piano lid — colors laid dark, saturated and velvety, never pale, never dirty.

Now the NEW subject, drawn in exactly that technique — a single small emblematic seal-like symbol, like a wax-seal crest or an ex-libris stamp, set alone in the middle of a mostly empty block: a single musical note with one bird's wing in place of its flag, drawn small and precise as an emblem or crest rather than a full illustrated scene, floating alone in the center of the block with generous calm dark rust-brown space all around it and nothing else — no staff, no ground, no horizon, no other object, no figure. The winged note itself is the entire content, treated like a printer's device or a seal impression. It is a sculptural object, not typography.

No text, no letters, no words anywhere — not even illegible squiggles. No signature, no edition number. No watermark, no sparkle or star symbol in any corner. Tender absurd humor, no kitsch. Portrait orientation 2:3.
