Study the attached reference lithographs by Adolf Born and imitate their TECHNIQUE precisely — not their subjects. Reproduce: the granular lithographic stipple texture covering every surface, the soft airbrushed color fields, the fine hatched line work, and the whimsical elongated caricature figures. The finished picture must look like it came from the same hand and the same portfolio as the references, at the same level of craft — the stipple laid dense, dark and rich rather than pale, every surface fully worked; do not simplify into a light cartoon line drawing. Everything stays matte and printmade — visible lithographic grain in every color field, no smooth digital shading, no gloss, no soft light effects, no glowing outlines. Print it on clean, fresh, evenly toned pale paper. NO FAKE AGING: no yellowed or browned paper, no stains, no foxing, no coffee marks, no dirt, no torn or deckled edges, no vignetting, no scratches, no creases, no simulated antique print. The lithographic grain is the technique; artificial age is not. Compose it as ONE central rectangular image block with slightly rounded corners, surrounded by a wide clean margin of fresh pale white paper on all four sides — the margin stays completely empty, no marginalia, no decoration. Palette of a 1920s lithographic poster: deep bottle green and near-black forest green dominating the scene, cold petrol teal as the second accent for the evening air over the valley, and warm ochres — the shouted voice itself is a band of warm ochre light and is the only bright thing in the picture — colors laid dark, saturated and velvety, never pale, never dirty.

The attached face crops define the mandatory facial grammar for every human being that appears anywhere in this picture, even the smallest silhouette: narrow elongated skulls, a long straight nose continuing the line of the forehead, small almond eyes set close, a small delicate mouth, fine hatching modelling the skin, pale flesh with a rose blush on the cheekbone. Never generic cartoon heads.

Now the NEW subject, drawn in exactly that technique — one single emblematic motif, not a narrative scene: two symbols facing each other across the dark block with a wide quiet gap between them, and the thing that happens between them. On the LEFT, small and low: the profile head of a boy with his mouth open in a shout, drawn as one compact dark emblem, not as a portrait — just the head, no body, no ground. On the RIGHT, large and heavy: the sheer dark silhouette of a mountain ridge, its edge cut hard against the sky. And between them, the interaction that the whole picture is about: one single band of warm ochre light travels from the boy's open mouth to the ridge as ONE line, strikes it, and comes back from the ridge as FOUR parallel lines fanning out toward the boy. The count is the point — one goes out, four come back. No halo, no glowing outlines, no light rays. Deep behind and far below, small and quiet along the bottom of the block: nothing at all — the two symbols float in quiet dark space, no valley floor, no houses, no horizon line. Nothing else — generous calm dark space around the central motif.

No text, no letters, no words anywhere — not even illegible squiggles. No signature, no edition number. No watermark, no sparkle or star symbol in any corner. Tender absurd humor, no kitsch. Square 1:1.
