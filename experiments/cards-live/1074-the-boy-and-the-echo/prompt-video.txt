Now the NEW subject, drawn in exactly that technique — a narrative poster scene with several things happening in it, read from the front of the picture to the back: at the front, on a strip of dark meadow grass at the very edge of a valley, a small boy of about seven stands with his back to us, his head tipped back and his mouth open in a shout, his fists clenched at his sides and one sleeve pulled up over his wrist. Sitting beside him on the grass, an old grandfather in a worn jacket and a soft cap, a pipe in the corner of his mouth, one thin column of smoke rising perfectly straight above him. Behind and below them the valley falls away: dark stepped meadows, a fence, one crooked hay rack, a lone spruce. Ringing the whole valley, filling the upper two thirds of the block, tall dark mountain walls of near-black forest green, ridge behind ridge. Out of the boy's mouth one band of warm ochre light flies out over the valley — and from every mountain wall at once, bands of the same warm ochre come streaming back toward him, four of them, five, arriving from all sides. High above, a small evening sky of cold petrol teal with one early star. Every element is separate and clearly readable, none of them overlapping into a blur.
