The finished picture must look like it came from the same hand and the same portfolio as the references, at the same level of craft — the stipple laid dense, dark and rich rather than pale, every surface fully worked; do not simplify into a light cartoon line drawing. Palette: deep dark sepia-brown, almost charcoal, dominating the scene, bottle green as the second accent, and warm golden ochres — one candle burning on the bedside table is the only real light in the whole comic, and its ochre glow is the only bright thing in every picture. Colors laid dark, saturated and velvety, never pale, never dirty.

CHARACTER CONSISTENCY — the LAST attached image is the character sheet for this comic. Match those four figures exactly, same faces, same clothes, same proportions. THE OLD MAN — very old, gaunt, lying or propped up in bed, a long white nightshirt, a bald freckled skull with a few white wisps, a heavy white moustache, narrow elongated skull, a long straight nose continuing the line of the forehead, small almond eyes set close together, a small delicate mouth, pale flesh with a rose blush on the cheekbone. THE PRIEST — tall and thin in a black cassock with a white collar, hollow cheeks, hair combed flat, always holding a small black book. THE DOCTOR — shorter and rounder, a dark three-piece suit, round spectacles, a neat grey beard, a stethoscope round his neck and a pocket watch in his hand. THE DOG — a lean old grey-muzzled hound with tall ears, tan-brown coat, sitting upright, calm knowing eyes. The wife, the son and the daughter are minor figures and follow the same facial grammar: narrow elongated skulls, long straight noses, small almond eyes set close, small delicate mouths. Never generic cartoon heads.
