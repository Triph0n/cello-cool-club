Study the attached reference lithographs by Adolf Born and imitate their TECHNIQUE precisely — not their subjects. Reproduce: the granular lithographic stipple texture covering every surface, the soft airbrushed color fields, the fine hatched line work, and the whimsical elongated caricature figures. The finished picture must look like it came from the same hand and the same portfolio as the references, at the same level of craft — the stipple laid dense, dark and rich rather than pale, every surface fully worked; do not simplify into a light cartoon line drawing. Everything stays matte and printmade — visible lithographic grain in every color field, no smooth digital shading, no gloss, no soft light effects, no glowing outlines. Print it on clean, fresh, evenly toned pale paper. NO FAKE AGING: no yellowed or browned paper, no stains, no foxing, no coffee marks, no dirt, no torn or deckled edges, no vignetting, no scratches, no creases, no simulated antique print. The lithographic grain is the technique; artificial age is not. Compose it as ONE central rectangular image block with slightly rounded corners, surrounded by a wide clean margin of fresh pale white paper on all four sides — the margin stays completely empty, no marginalia, no decoration. Palette of a 1920s lithographic poster: midnight blue dominating, bottle green forests and oceans, warm ochre lit windows, one bordeaux roof — colors laid dark, saturated and velvety, never pale, never dirty.

The attached face crops define the mandatory facial grammar for every face in this picture, human or belonging to a personified planet, even the smallest one: narrow elongated skulls, a long straight nose continuing the line of the forehead, small almond eyes set close, a small delicate mouth, fine hatching modelling the skin, a rose blush on the cheekbone. Never generic cartoon heads.

Now the NEW subject, drawn in exactly that technique — one single emblematic motif, not a narrative scene: THE WHOLE PLANET, one complete sphere floating in the dark and filling most of the image block, drawn as a gentle personified character: the noisy house Earth, her whole blue-green sphere alive and messy and magnificent: oceans that dream, tiny murmuring forests, small mountains scratching cloud bellies — and clinging to the top of the sphere a small crooked tenement house with every window lit warm ochre, laundry lines with tiny shirts, a smoking chimney, a tiny dog barking from the roof — the only inhabited house on the whole street of the sun. The sphere must be entire and unbroken — no cropping by the block edge, no horizon cutting it. No halo, no glowing outlines, no light rays. Far behind in the dark a straight row of smooth silent marbles — her clean and tidy sisters, perfect and empty. Nothing else — generous calm dark space around the planet.

No text, no letters, no words anywhere — not even illegible squiggles. No signature, no edition number. No watermark, no sparkle or star symbol in any corner. Tender absurd humor, no kitsch. Portrait orientation 2:3.
