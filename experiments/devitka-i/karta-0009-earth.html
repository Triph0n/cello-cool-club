Nine I · 3/9 · The Celestial Household

Earth, the Noisy House

After my sisters, the burned and the burning,

I am the third, the lucky one

The blue marble in the great silent game

I am not made of fire nor of poison

I am made of water, of earth, and of patience

And of a very strange thing called life

I carry on my back oceans that dream

Forests that murmur old secrets

And mountains that scratch the belly of the clouds

I have thousands of creatures

Those that crawl, those that swim, those that fly

And then I have my strangest children

Men

They have a mad talent for beauty

They know how to make a stone or a piece of wood speak

They write poems that make me weep with joy

They invent music that makes the stars dance

They know the taste of warm bread

The warmth of a friendly hand

And the sweetness of a kiss in the rain

They are my magnificent children

But they also have a mad talent for foolishness

They draw lines on my skin and call them borders

They wage war over pieces of land that belong to me

They build factories that make me cough up black smoke

They cover my meadows with cement and sadness

They speak of love and they make bombs

They are so intelligent

And so incredibly stupid

They are my despairing children

So I turn in the great silence

The only inhabited house on the street of the sun

The only planet with laughter and wars

With songs and lies

With libraries and prisons

My distant sisters are clean and tidy

They are perfect in their hell or their solitude

I am messy, I am full of scars

I am noisy and dirty and magnificent and sick

I am their mother, their home, their ship

And I wait

I wait for them to grow up a little

I wait for them to understand

That the only border that matters

Is the one between tenderness and cruelty.
