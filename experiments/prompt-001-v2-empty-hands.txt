Study the attached lithographs by Adolf Born and imitate their TECHNIQUE precisely — not their subjects. Reproduce: the granular lithographic stipple texture, the soft airbrushed color fields, the fine hatched line work, the whimsical elongated caricature figures, the aged warm paper, and the overall feel of a precious old print. Frame the scene with a theatre proscenium: heavy draped curtains at the sides and wooden stage boards below, like a play being performed. Richer joyful saturated colors with one glowing accent of vermilion.

Now the NEW subject, drawn in exactly that technique: in the middle of an old market square stands a monument on a tall stone plinth — but the monument is nothing except TWO ENORMOUS EMPTY OPEN HANDS, palms turned upward, carved from pale weathered stone, completely empty, holding nothing at all. Grey pigeons swarm all over the giant stone hands: perched along every finger, nesting in the hollow of each palm, wheeling in the air around them, dozens and dozens of them. Around the base of the plinth teems a crowd of tiny officials in bowler hats and dark frock coats: one climbs a ladder to peer into the empty stone palm looking for crumbs, one measures a fingertip with a tape, several unroll long paper scrolls of regulations across the cobblestones, one holds up a huge red rubber stamp with nothing to stamp, one points accusingly at the sky, a small dog sniffs a scroll. At the very foot of the plinth, small and calm among all the commotion, a thin old man in a grey coat sits on a bench with his own hands open and empty in his lap, pigeons on his shoulders and hat, smiling faintly. A vermilion market awning glows at the edge of the square. Behind, a hazy town hall with one open window.
