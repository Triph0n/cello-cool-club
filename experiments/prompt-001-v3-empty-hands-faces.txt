Study the attached lithographs by Adolf Born and imitate their TECHNIQUE precisely — not their subjects. Reproduce: the granular lithographic stipple texture, the soft airbrushed color fields, the fine hatched line work, and the whimsical elongated caricature figures. Print it on clean, fresh, evenly toned pale paper. NO FAKE AGING: no yellowed or browned paper, no stains, no foxing, no coffee marks, no dirt, no torn or deckled edges, no vignetting, no scratches, no creases, no simulated antique print. The lithographic grain is the technique; artificial age is not. Frame the scene with a theatre proscenium: heavy draped curtains at the sides and wooden stage boards below, like a play being performed. Richer joyful saturated colors with one glowing accent of vermilion.

The attached face crops define the mandatory facial grammar for EVERY human being in this picture — main figures and the smallest background figures alike. Follow them exactly: narrow elongated skulls, a long straight nose continuing the line of the forehead, small almond eyes set close, a small delicate mouth, fine hatching modelling the skin, pale flesh with a rose blush on the cheekbone, refined 19th-century-theatre bearing. Draw these faces, not generic cartoon heads. They are face TYPES, not recurring characters — vary age, hair and expression freely.

Now the NEW subject, drawn in exactly that technique: in the middle of an old market square stands a monument on a tall stone plinth — but the monument is nothing except TWO ENORMOUS EMPTY OPEN HANDS, palms turned upward, carved from pale weathered stone, completely empty, holding nothing at all. Grey pigeons swarm all over the giant stone hands: perched along every finger, nesting in the hollow of each palm, wheeling in the air around them, dozens and dozens of them. Around the base of the plinth teems a crowd of tiny officials in dark frock coats and bowler hats, each with a long narrow face from the attached crops: one climbs a ladder to peer into the empty stone palm through a magnifying glass, one measures a fingertip with a tape, several unroll long blank paper scrolls across the cobblestones, one holds up a huge vermilion rubber stamp with nothing to stamp, one points accusingly at the sky, a small dog sniffs a scroll. At the very foot of the plinth, small and calm among all the commotion, a thin old man in a grey coat sits on a bench with his own hands open and empty in his lap, pigeons on his shoulders and hat, smiling faintly — his face is the bearded elderly face from the attached crops. A vermilion market awning glows at the edge of the square. Behind, a hazy town hall with one open window.

No text, no letters, no words anywhere — not even illegible squiggles on the documents or scrolls, they are completely blank. No signature, no edition number. Tender absurd humor, no kitsch. Portrait orientation 2:3.
