CHANGE ONE THING — how the tone is made. Do not use smooth even mechanical stipple. Every tone in this picture is built from INK BLOWN AND SPATTERED onto the paper: ink flicked from a loaded brush and blown through a straw. The whole surface is therefore covered in a fine SPRAY of countless ROUND INK SPOTS of many different sizes — the great majority as fine as dust, many the size of a pinhead, and only a scattering of larger blots. Crucially, THE SPRAY IS THE SHADING: it is packed dense and overlapping in the deep shadows, thinning gradually across the mid-tones, and drifting apart into single stray specks in the lit areas — so the spots build every gradient, they are not sprinkled evenly like dirt on top of a finished picture. Vary them constantly: most are grey, sepia, dusty blue or faded rather than solid black, many are translucent, some are pale rings with lighter centres, some have soft bled edges where the ink was still wet, some sit above the colour washes and others sink beneath them. Fine tails and satellite specks trail from the larger blots where the ink was blown across the paper. Together with the ink hatching this spattered spray carries all the modelling. The paper itself stays clean, fresh and evenly toned — the spatter is deliberate ink, NOT dirt or age: no yellowing, no staining, no foxing, no torn or deckled edges, no simulated antique print. Layout as in the references: a single deep scene inside an image block with softly rounded corners, floating on a clean ivory margin.
